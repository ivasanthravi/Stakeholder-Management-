# Getting started with Meridian

## 1. Create your account
Go to **Sign up** and enter your name, work email and a password of at least 10 characters. If you were invited, use the link in your invitation — your email is filled in for you.

## 2. Verify your email
Open the link we email you. You can't create or join a workspace until your email is verified. No email? Check spam, then use **Resend link** on the "Check your email" page.

## 3. Create a workspace and choose a template
Name your workspace and pick one of 8 industry templates. A template sets your vocabulary (e.g. "Project" instead of "Initiative", "Client" instead of "Organization"), engagement types and organization types; a preview shows exactly what it changes. You can rename the vocabulary later.

Your workspace starts a **14-day free trial of Professional for up to 10 people. No card required.** If someone has already invited you to a workspace, it's listed here so you can join it instead.

## 4. Guided setup
Four short steps, each skippable ("Skip for now", or "Skip setup" to leave). Progress is based on your data, so if you leave and come back you resume at the first unfinished step.
1. **Workspace** — rename it and adjust the three vocabulary words.
2. **First initiative** — a name and a short code.
3. **Stakeholders** — add up to 8 people (name and title), linked to that initiative.
4. **Invite teammates** — emails and roles. The seat meter shows how many seats you have left.

## 5. Invite your team
Settings › Members (Administrators and Owners). Invitations expire after 7 days and can be resent (the old link stops working) or revoked. Seats used = active members + pending invitations. The invitee must sign in with the invited email address.

## 6. Roles
| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| Owner | Everything, including billing and deleting the workspace |
| Administrator | Manage members, roles and settings; see restricted records |
| Manager | Run initiatives and decisions; archive records |
| Contributor | Add and update stakeholders, engagements and commitments |
| Viewer | Read non-restricted records |

A workspace always keeps at least one Owner. Settings › Roles & permissions shows the full matrix.

## 7. Your first records
- **Stakeholder:** press `C` then `P` (or ⌘K → New stakeholder). Add role/title, organization, influence (1–4, your team's judgement), stance, interest, owner and tags.
- **Engagement:** `C` then `E`. Log a meeting, call, email, workshop, consultation, site visit or note, with participants and notes.
- **Commitment:** `C` then `M`. Record a promise made by you or to you, with an owner and due date.
- **Decisions** move through proposed → under review → approved / rejected / deferred, with approvers, consulted stakeholders and a needed-by date.

## 8. Command Center (Home)
Rule-based signals: overdue and due-soon commitments, decisions waiting, cooling relationships, stance shifts, unowned high-influence stakeholders, unassigned and repeatedly delayed commitments. Each one shows why it's flagged, the rule, the evidence and a suggested next step.

## 9. Relationship map
`G` then `M`. Record relationships (reports to, influences, allied with, opposes, …) and explore them as a network or an influence grid. **Find path** (Professional) shows the shortest chain of recorded relationships, up to 4 steps. It shows recorded links; it doesn't measure influence.

## 10. AI features
AI features run on a **built-in rules engine, or on a large language model when enabled** for your environment. Each result shows which engine produced it.
- **Meeting intelligence** (Professional): on an engagement, analyse notes or upload a transcript (.txt, .md, .vtt, .srt). Meridian proposes decisions, commitments and actions, each tied to a quote from the source. **Nothing is saved until you accept it** — accept, edit, reject or merge each item.
- **Briefings**: stakeholder (brief, detailed, meeting prep), decision and workspace briefings.
- **Copilot** (⌘J): ask questions about records you can access; answers cite their sources.

With the rules engine only, copilot answers a set of common question patterns and briefings show facts, metrics and rules without written interpretation. See [known-issues.md](known-issues.md).
